Lime-on-black, light, and customer accents

Lime-on-black is the strongest brand asset the product has, and every frame in the pack proves it reads as one coherent thing. It should stay as the default. But it is currently the only option, and three real situations break it: reading an atlas in a bright room, pasting a frame into a doc or a light-mode README, and embedding an atlas inside a customer's own product.

None of those need a second design system. They need the accent to become a slot and the background/text pair to be flippable. The neutral ramp, the spacing, the type, and the C4 kind hues all stay exactly where they are.

The delta, in tokens

--atlas-bg#070a0b → #f6f8f7
--atlas-text#f1f7f4 → #101a17
--atlas-accent#d9ff70 → #3f7d1f
--atlas-panel-solid#0d1213 → #ffffff
--atlas-line-stronglight-on-dark → dark-on-light
--atlas-bluekind hue · darkened only
  • Dark stays the defaultIt is the product's signature and it survives every frame in the pack. Nothing here proposes replacing it.
  • Light is a distribution requirement, not a preferencePublic share URLs, oEmbed cards, and screenshots pasted into docs all land on light surfaces today. A dark-only atlas arrives as a black hole in someone else's page.
  • The accent must become a slotOnce --atlas-accent means exactly one thing — “the node you are on” — swapping it per tenant is a one-token change with no layout consequence. The four tiles above differ only in that token.
  • Kind hues survive both themesPerson, container, component, and external system need to stay distinguishable in light mode; the cyan/blue/purple/orange ramp only needs darkening, not re-authoring.
  • Contrast is the real bug--atlas-line at 10% alpha on #070a0b is why the self-scan boxes vanish in 08b. Whatever happens to theming, resting borders need more contrast than they have now.
  • Out of scope hereThis frame does not retokenize apps/web/src/app.css and does not teach the canvas renderer about themes. Both are real follow-on work; the token seam is what is being argued for.
